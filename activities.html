Practice Tasks

Activities

Apply the concepts from each lesson through short analysis, design, and planning tasks.

01

Animation Through Ages

Research Task · Individual Activity

Create a timeline showing the development of animation. Include the early optical devices, important milestones, and changes brought by digital animation.

Q1.
What are the differences between the thaumatrope, phenakistoscope, zoetrope, and flipbook?
Q2.
Which historical development had the greatest effect on animation? Explain your choice.
Q3.
How did digital tools change the way animators create and present animation?
📎 Submission Note

Submit your timeline as a hand-drawn chart or a digital layout. Add dates and short explanations for each milestone.

02

Plan Your Animation Studio

Planning Task · Individual / Pair Activity

Develop a simple profile for a fictional animation studio. Define its identity, purpose, target audience, and the people needed to complete a production.

Q1.
What name and brand identity would you give your animation studio?
Q2.
Write one vision statement and one mission statement for your studio.
Q3.
Who is your target audience, and what roles would your production team need?
03

Visual Design and Color Board

Creative Task · Individual Activity

Create a visual board for a character, object, or scene. Apply the elements of design, choose a suitable color scheme, and use typography to communicate a clear mood.

Design Checklist

✔
Use at least four visual elements, such as line, shape, form, color, space, or texture.
✔
Choose a color scheme and keep the colors consistent with the intended mood.
✔
Use readable typography with a clear visual hierarchy.
✔
Arrange the elements so the main subject stands out.
Q1.
Which visual elements did you use, and how do they support your design?
Q2.
What color scheme and typeface did you choose? Explain your choices.
04

Image Format and Storyboard Practice

Performance Task · Individual Activity

Prepare a short storyboard with six panels. For each panel, identify the camera shot and describe the action. Then choose a suitable image format for the artwork you plan to use.

Q1.
What is the difference between raster and vector images?
Q2.
When would you choose JPEG, PNG, GIF, or HEIC for an image? Explain your choices.
Q3.
Which camera shots did you use in your storyboard, and what does each shot communicate?
Q4.
How does the order of panels help viewers understand the beginning, middle, and end?
⭐ Grading Guide

Your output will be assessed based on accuracy of lesson concepts (30%), clarity of visual planning (30%), creativity and organization (20%), and completeness (20%).
